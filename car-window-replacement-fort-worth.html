What Does Car Window Replacement Involve?

For a door window, the tech takes off the door panel, removes the broken pieces from inside the door and vacuums the glass out of the seats, carpet and window channel. The new glass bolts to the regulator and gets tested up and down.

Back glass and fixed quarter glass are set in urethane, much like a windshield. Back glass with defroster lines gets its connectors reattached and checked.

When Do You Need a Car Window Replaced?

  • A break-in left a door window shattered.
  • The back glass blew out from a rock, a tailgate slam or a temperature change.
  • A window dropped into the door and won’t come back up.
  • A truck slider is cracked or leaking.

Why Do Side and Back Windows Shatter Instead of Crack?

Side and back windows are usually tempered glass, which breaks into small cube-shaped pieces when it fails. Windshields are laminated, with a plastic layer that holds the cracked glass together.

That is why a broken side window can’t be repaired; it has to be replaced. It also means there is glass everywhere, including deep inside the door, so the cleanup is part of the job. “Can you get the glass out of my seats?” is the first thing most people ask, and yes, we do. Some newer cars use laminated side glass for noise and security, which costs more.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you replace a car window the same day?

Same-day car window replacement in Fort Worth is often possible when the glass is in stock locally. Less common windows, laminated side glass and some back glass may take a day or two to arrive.

Do you clean up the broken glass?

Yes. Every car window replacement in Fort Worth includes vacuuming the broken glass out of the seats, floor and door. Glass can hide in seat tracks, so we take our time on it.

Does insurance cover a broken window from a break-in?

A window broken in a break-in is usually covered under comprehensive coverage on a Texas auto policy, minus your deductible. Items stolen from the car generally fall under homeowners or renters insurance instead.
